ADHD and keeping in touch with friends

You mean to message them soon, and then it’s been five months.

If you have ADHD you may know the pattern. You think of a friend and mean to reply, then something else takes over and the weeks go by without you noticing. When you do remember, it’s been so long that getting in touch feels awkward, so you put it off again. Lots of people with ADHD describe this, and it says nothing about how much they love their friends.

OhHey is a private iPhone and Android app that does the remembering for you.

Why friendships drift

A friend who isn’t in front of you can slip out of your thoughts completely, even one you adore.

Time gets fuzzy, too. “A couple of weeks ago” turns out to have been March, and without a real number it’s hard to tell who you saw recently and who you haven’t seen all year.

The gap makes the message harder. After two weeks, “how are you?” is easy. After six months it seems to need an apology, an explanation and a plan, so it doesn’t get sent.

Then there’s getting started. Choosing who to contact, what to say and when to meet all take energy, and an empty message box is easy to put down.

Apps that nag can make it worse. Streaks, red badges and a pile of overdue reminders leave you feeling behind, and then you avoid the app as well as the friend.

What helps

  • Friends you can see, so remembering doesn’t rely on memory.
  • A real number: “seven weeks” tells you more than “a while”.
  • A small first step, like one message to one friend.
  • Reminders that come one at a time.
  • No streak to lose when you miss a week.
  • A note of the little things, so you know what to ask next time.

How OhHey helps

Friends stay in view
Every friend has two rings round their face, one for meeting up and one for keeping in touch. The rings drain as the days pass and turn orange when you’re drifting, and the face starts to fade. Widgets put those faces on your home screen.
Real numbers
Each friend shows how long it’s been and when you’re next due: “Due in 4 weeks”, “Due 3 days ago”.
Each friend at their own pace
Say how often you’d like to meet up and how often just to message, separately for each friend.
One nudge about one friend
At a time you choose, only on days someone’s due, and never the same face three days running while anyone else is due. Birthdays, and anyone you ask to be reminded about, get a reminder on the day.
A first message ready
Each nudge comes with an opener you can send as it is or change. One tap opens WhatsApp, iMessage, Signal, Telegram, your texts or a call.
Logging in one tap
Tap Met up or In touch in the app, on the widget or on the reminder, and the rings fill back up. On Android, OhHey can also notice when a friend messages you and count that as being in touch. It never reads what they said.
A weekly check-in
Once a week, on the day you choose, it asks who you caught up with. Tick the people you saw or spoke to; it takes about ten seconds.
Notes for next time
Jot down the new job or the house move after you meet, and the next reminder shows “Last time: new job at the studio”.
I’m free
Got an evening? It suggests who’s nearby, up for it and due a catch-up.
Taking a break
Pause reminders when life is full, and come back to a calm “welcome back” instead of a backlog. If a friend is going through something hard, you can mark them for gentle check-ins more often for a while.

What OhHey won’t do

  • There are no streaks, points or scores.
  • Reminders never arrive as a pile of overdue friends. They come one at a time.
  • It doesn’t read your messages.
  • It has no account and no ads, and your friends never leave your phone.

Questions

Is OhHey a treatment for ADHD?

No. It’s a reminder app for keeping in touch with friends, not a medical product, and it’s no substitute for advice or support from a professional.

Do my friends need the app?

No. Only you use OhHey, and it opens the messengers you already have.

Will it make me feel guilty?

It’s designed not to. There are no streaks to break, reminders come one friend at a time, and you can snooze anyone or pause everything.

Is it free?

Yes, with every feature, for your closest six friends plus the people you live with. Premium (£9.99 a year, or £24.99 once) makes room for everyone else.

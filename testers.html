Testing

Thanks for trying OhHey early.

You’re getting a test version before it’s in the App Store and Google Play. It works like the real thing, but you might spot the odd rough edge. That’s exactly what’s useful to hear about.

On an iPhone

  1. Install TestFlight from the App Store. It’s Apple’s app for test versions, and it’s free.
  2. Open the TestFlight link you were sent, on your iPhone.
  3. Tap Accept, then Install. OhHey appears on your home screen like any other app.

New test versions show up in TestFlight, which can update them for you. Test versions stop working after 90 days, by which time there’ll be a newer one.

On an Android phone

  1. Open the download link you were sent, on your phone, and download the file (it ends in .apk).
  2. Tap the file to open it. If your phone asks, allow your browser or Files app to install unknown apps. That’s just because it isn’t from Google Play yet.
  3. Tap Install. If Google Play Protect says it doesn’t recognise the app, tap More details, then Install anyway.

For a newer version, do the same again. It installs over the old one and keeps everything you’ve added.

Your friends stay on your phone

Everything you add stays on your phone, just as it will in the real app. Deleting the test version deletes it too, so if you want to keep it, turn on Settings → Back up to Google Drive (or iCloud on an iPhone) first. If you reinstall it or move to a new phone, choose Restore from a backup as OhHey opens.

If the app crashes, it sends a crash report so the problem can be fixed. A report says what went wrong in the code, never anything about you or your friends. You can switch it off in Settings → Privacy. The privacy policy has the details.

Worth trying

  • Add a few close friends, from your contacts or by hand, and choose how often you’d like to see each one and how often just to be in touch.
  • Tap Met up when you see someone, and message someone straight from their page: WhatsApp, iMessage, a text or a call.
  • Make a plan with one friend or several, with a time and a place. Switch on All day for a weekend away or a wedding, up to 14 days. Already booked? From my calendar turns an event in your phone’s calendar into a plan.
  • Got an evening spare? Try I’m free on the Home tab, and Who’s around? to see who’s nearby.
  • Jot a note after you meet up, and pin a few About them facts, like their kids’ names.
  • Add birthdays, and you’ll get a reminder on the day with a message ready.
  • Busy few weeks? Settings → Reminders → Take a break pauses reminders until you’re back.
  • Press and hold the OhHey icon on your home screen for shortcuts, like logging a catch-up straight away.
  • Not sure what something means? Settings → How OhHey works explains the rings and the rest.

The widget

The OhHey widget, Your circle, puts the friends who are drifting or due, birthdays and plans on your home screen. Tap a face to open that friend. It keeps itself up to date; if it ever looks behind, open OhHey once.

On an iPhone

  1. Press and hold an empty space on your home screen until the apps start to wiggle.
  2. Tap Edit in the top corner, then Add Widget (on older iPhones, tap the + button instead).
  3. Search for OhHey and swipe to pick a size. Small shows the one friend who most needs a hello; medium and large show more faces, with birthdays and plans.
  4. Tap Add Widget, move it where you like, then tap Done.

It works on the lock screen too: press and hold the lock screen, tap Customise, choose the lock screen, then tap the space under the clock and pick OhHey.

On an Android phone

  1. Press and hold an empty space on your home screen.
  2. Tap Widgets, and find OhHey in the list (or search for it).
  3. Press and hold Your circle and drag it onto your home screen.
  4. To make it bigger or smaller, press and hold it, then drag its edges.

Reminders

OhHey only reminds you when there’s a reason to: a nudge about one friend on days someone’s due, at a time you choose, plus birthdays, a weekly check-in, and “How did it go?” after a plan. Choose which ones you want in Settings → Reminders.

  • Let them through. When OhHey asks to send notifications, tap Allow. If you said no, you can turn them on in your phone’s Settings: on an iPhone, Notifications → OhHey; on Android, Apps → OhHey → Notifications.
  • Use the buttons. A reminder has buttons such as Message, Met up and Snooze a week. On Android, swipe down on the reminder or tap its arrow to see them; on an iPhone, press and hold it.
  • On Android, make sure they come on time. Android can hold reminders back by minutes or hours. If Settings → Reminders says Reminders may arrive late, tap it and allow Alarms & reminders. If they still come late, set OhHey’s battery use to Unrestricted in your phone’s Apps → OhHey → Battery (the wording differs a little between phones).
  • Try one now. Settings → Testing → Send a test reminder sends one in a minute, so you can check it arrives and try the buttons.

Test versions also have a Testing section at the bottom of Settings. You can ignore it.

Tell us what you think

Anything confusing, broken or missing, or anything you like: message whoever sent you the link, or use Settings → Send feedback, which fills in which version you have. A screenshot helps.

After a week or so, it helps a lot to send Settings → Testing → Usage summary → Share. It’s a short list of how often and for how long you used each part of the app, and what opened it (a reminder, the widget or the app icon), counted on your phone. It has no names, notes or anything you’ve typed, and it only goes to whoever you send it to.

Fixes often arrive by themselves: close OhHey and open it again, and the next time you open it you’ll have the latest.

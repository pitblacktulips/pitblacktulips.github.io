It’s not that you don’t care.

Weeks turn into months, and then it feels awkward to get in touch. It happens to anyone with a busy life, and especially to people with ADHD. OhHey does the remembering, so you can do the caring.

Reading the rings

Two rings round every friend.

Each ring is a countdown. It starts full when you catch up, and empties as the days pass, until it’s time to get in touch again.

  • The outer ring is for meeting up. Full when you’ve just seen them, empty when it’s time to meet again: every month, say.
  • The inner ring is for keeping in touch. A message, a call, a voice note. It runs on its own, shorter clock: every week, say.
  • Green plenty of time
  • Blue due within a week
  • Orange drifting, and their face starts to fade

Try it in the box at the top: drag Skip ahead to watch the rings drain, then tap a face to log a catch-up and fill them up again.

Questions

Good to know.

Anything else? Email appohhey@gmail.com.

Do my friends need the app?

No. Only you use OhHey. It opens WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram or your texts to message them.

Does it read my messages?

No. On Android it can notice who messaged you, so it counts as being in touch. It never reads what they said.

Will it nag me?

No. At most one gentle nudge a day, about one friend. You can snooze anyone, or take a break from reminders altogether.

Is it good for people with ADHD?

It was designed with ADHD in mind: friends stay in view, real numbers instead of “a while”, and no streaks or guilt.

What if I get a new phone?

Restore from your own iCloud or Google Drive backup, and everything comes back.

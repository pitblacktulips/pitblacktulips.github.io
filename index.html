How it works

A calm nudge, not another to-do list.

Add the people you want to see more, how often you’d like to, and how much notice they need. OhHey does the remembering.

Your circle

See who’s drifting, at a glance.

Open the app and you’ll see who you’ve caught up with this month, who’s due soon, and who it’s been too long with. Free right now? It’s the first thing on the screen.

  • Weekly, monthly, every 10 days: you set the rhythm for each person
  • Message them in one tap, on WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram, text or a call
  • Everyone up to date? OhHey says so, and leaves you be

I’m free

Got a free evening? See who’s around.

Tap “I’m free” and OhHey suggests who to message: people nearby, up for it at that notice, and due a catch-up. Planning further ahead? Pick a day and it finds the friends who need booking in.

  • Now, tonight, this weekend, or weeks ahead
  • See everyone on a map
  • Away for a few days? Set a trip and see who’s near there

Meet up, or keep in touch

Two rhythms for every friend.

Some friends you want to see every month. Others you’d be happy just to message now and then. Each friend has a meet-up rhythm and a keep-in-touch rhythm, and OhHey counts down both.

  • Any message, call or catch-up counts as keeping in touch
  • A pen pal? Turn off meet-up reminders for them
  • Pick a custom rhythm, like every 3 weeks

Plans and birthdays

Plans, countdowns, and every birthday.

See who’s overdue and how many days are left for everyone else. The calendar shows your plans and every birthday for the year ahead.

  • Add plans straight to your phone’s calendar
  • On someone’s birthday, a message is ready to send
  • “How did it go?” after each plan, and a look back at each month

Friends abroad

Find a good time to talk.

For friends in other time zones, OhHey shows what time it is where they live and suggests good times to call, worked out from their time zone and the usual hours you add. No tracking, just the clock.

  • Add their usual working hours if you know them, and yours
  • A gentle check before you message at 3am their time

Out of sight, out of mind?

It’s not that you don’t care.

If friends slip your mind the moment they’re not in front of you, you’re not alone. It happens to anyone with a busy life, and it’s especially familiar to people with ADHD, who often describe it as struggling with object permanence. Weeks turn into months, then it feels awkward to get in touch. OhHey was designed with that in mind.

Always in view

A home-screen widget shows your friends’ faces, with a ring that empties as it’s been a while. Friends don’t vanish the moment you look away.

Real numbers, not a vague “a while”

“8 days left” or “2 weeks overdue” for everyone. Handy when it’s hard to judge how long it’s been.

It picks for you

Free tonight? Tap “I’m free” and it suggests who to message. No staring at your contacts, no decision fatigue.

One tap, then done

Log a catch-up in a tap, even straight from the notification. On Android it can notice when friends message you on WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram or by text. Made a mistake? Undo.

Remember what they told you

Add a quick note after you meet up: the new job, the house move. Read it before you see them next.

No guilt, no streaks

No streaks to break and no scolding. Just a friendly “Oh hey, Sam’s due a catch-up” when it’s been longer than you’d like.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

How much does it cost?

OhHey is free for up to 6 friends (people you live with don’t count), with every feature. OhHey Premium removes the limit for £3.49 a year (or the same in your currency). No ads, ever.

Is it good for people with ADHD?

Yes. It’s for anyone whose friendships slip when life gets busy, but it was designed with ADHD in mind too: it keeps people in view, cuts down decisions, and never guilt-trips you.

What if I’d rather just message some friends?

That’s fine. Every friend has two rhythms: how often to meet up, and how often to keep in touch. Turn off meet-up reminders for a pen pal and OhHey will only nudge you to say hello.

Does it work on iPhone and Android?

Yes, both. A couple of extras differ: on Android, OhHey can optionally notice when a friend messages you on WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram or by text. iPhones don’t allow that, so on iPhone you log contact with a tap instead.

Does it read my messages?

No. OhHey never reads message text. The optional Android feature only looks at who a message notification is from, so it can note that you were in touch. It’s off unless you turn it on.

Do my friends need the app?

No. It’s just for you. Your friends don’t get anything from OhHey; when you message them, it opens WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram or your normal messages like you’d expect.

Where is my data kept?

Only on your phone. There’s no OhHey account or server.

When you type a postcode or town, just that text is looked up to place it on a map. The privacy policy has the details.

What if I get a new phone?

OhHey saves a backup each week, and your phone’s iCloud or Google backup keeps it, so it comes across when you move phones. You can also go to Settings → Save a copy elsewhere any time, and choose Restore from a backup on the new phone.

What do “Spontaneous”, “Same day” and “Book ahead” mean?

How much notice each friend needs. Spontaneous friends are up for it within the hour; book-ahead friends need a couple of weeks. OhHey uses it to suggest the right people at the right time.

Will it nag me?

Only if you ask it to. Reminders are off until you turn them on, and you can choose which: a daily nudge when someone’s overdue, a Sunday planning nudge, birthdays, a monthly look back, or a check-in after plans. You can log a catch-up straight from the notification.

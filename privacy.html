Privacy policy

Your friends stay on your phone.

Last updated 30 October 2026

The short version

OhHey has no account, no server of its own, no ads and no analytics. Everything you add (friends, plans, notes, catch-ups) is stored only on your device. We don’t collect it, can’t see it and don’t sell it. The only things that leave your phone for us are a crash report if the app breaks (what went wrong in the code, nothing about you or your friends, and you can switch it off) and, if you subscribe to Premium, a record of that subscription.

What the app stores, and where

The friends, rough areas, plans, birthdays, photos and notes you add are saved in the app’s private storage on your phone. If you add the home-screen widget, the few details it shows (names, photos and who’s due) are kept on the phone for the widget too. Deleting the app, or using Settings → Delete all data, removes them.

When information leaves your phone

  • Looking up a place. When you type a postcode or town, or add a friend from your contacts who has an address, just that postcode or town is sent to postcodes.io or your phone’s built-in location service to find it on a map. No names or other details go with it.
  • Using your location. If you tap “Use where I am”, your phone works out your rough area once. It isn’t tracked or kept as a live location.
  • Maps. Maps come from Google Maps on Android and Apple Maps on iPhone, which load map images from their services. Your friends’ details aren’t sent to them.
  • Messaging, calls and calendars. When you message or call someone, or add a plan to your calendar, OhHey opens WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram, Messages, your phone or your calendar app with the details filled in. Those apps handle it under their own policies.
  • Backups. Each week OhHey saves a backup copy in its own storage on your phone, which your phone’s iCloud or Google backup may include, under your account with Apple or Google. You can also save a backup file yourself, for example to Google Drive, iCloud or email. It goes only where you send it.
  • Backing up to Google Drive or iCloud (optional). If you turn this on in Settings, OhHey saves a copy of everything you’ve added, photos included, to your own Google Drive (Android) or iCloud (iPhone), as often as you choose. It goes straight from your phone to your account, in a private folder only OhHey can open: it doesn’t appear among your files, and we never see it. On Android you choose a Google account to back up to; OhHey asks Google only for permission to use that private folder, so it can’t see anything else in your Drive. Apple and Google store the backup under their own policies. To remove it, turn backups off and delete OhHey’s data in your Google Drive settings (Manage apps) or your iPhone’s iCloud settings.
  • OhHey Premium. If you subscribe, Apple or Google takes the payment; we never see your card or bank details, name or email. To know whether Premium is active, the app uses RevenueCat, which receives a random ID created for this install (not linked to you), your purchase and renewal history, your country and the app version. That’s all it gets: no friends, contacts or anything you’ve typed. It’s used only to unlock Premium and restore it on a new phone.
  • App updates. When it opens, OhHey checks with Expo, the service it’s built and updated with, whether a newer version of its code is available, so fixes arrive without waiting for the App Store or Google Play. That check sends the app’s version and platform, nothing about you or your friends.
  • Crash reports. If OhHey crashes, it can send a report to Sentry, the service we use to find and fix crashes. A report contains the error, where in the app’s code it happened, which screens were open just before, the app version and the phone’s model and operating system. It never includes your friends, notes, plans, contacts, anything you’ve typed, your location, your name or your phone’s name, and it isn’t used to track you. Reports are kept for up to 90 days. You can turn them off in Settings → Privacy → Send crash reports.

Permissions

OhHey asks for each of these only when you use the feature that needs it. Saying no just turns that feature off.

  • Contacts, if you choose to add friends from your contacts. OhHey reads your contacts’ names, numbers, photos, addresses, favourites and birthdays to show you the list. Only the people you pick are saved in OhHey; the rest are not kept.
  • Photos and camera, if you choose a photo for a friend. The photo stays on your phone.
  • Location, only if you tap “Use where I am”.
  • Calendar, only to add a plan you’ve made.
  • Notifications, only if you turn on reminders.
  • Notification access (Android, optional). If you turn on “Notice messages from friends”, OhHey looks at who a WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram or text message notification is from and, if it matches a friend, notes that you were in touch that day. It keeps only the names and times of the last few chats it noticed, on your phone, so you can see what was picked up. It never reads or stores message text, and group chats are ignored. You can turn it off at any time.

Your choices

Because everything is on your phone, you’re in control: edit or delete any friend, plan or note in the app, turn off crash reports, reminders, message noticing or cloud backups in Settings, or delete everything with Settings → Delete all data. As we don’t hold your data, there’s nothing for us to export or delete on our side, apart from crash reports, which contain nothing about you and expire on their own, and a Premium subscription record, which we can delete on request.

Children

OhHey isn’t aimed at children and doesn’t knowingly collect anyone’s information.
